0. ONE-PARAGRAPH PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bharat Learners are students aged 18-24 in Tier-2/3 Indian cities who mostly
use regional languages; about 7M of them use ChatGPT weekly. Most know ChatGPT
Voice exists (~80%) and have tried it (~85%), but only ~19-20% use it often.
Demand and awareness are not what holds them back. Nothing in their study flow
tells them WHEN voice beats typing, so they fall back on typing (~70% "prefer
typing"). The voice entry point is small and gives no reason to use it, and
users have doubts about accents and privacy. As a result, ChatGPT fails to turn
the voice habit these users already have (WhatsApp voice notes, YouTube voice
search) into a study habit. The proposed solution, Smart Voice Suggestions,
spots voice-friendly learning tasks (mock interviews, spoken English practice,
viva/oral revision, pronunciation) and offers a one-tap, task-preset voice
session in context, below the normal text answer. The goal is to grow Weekly
Active Voice Learners (WAVL) and D7 voice retention without hurting text-chat
quality.

CORE INSIGHT: Voice has a retention/activation problem, not an awareness
problem. "Demand exists. Activation is broken." "Availability != habit adoption."

PART A -- MILESTONE 1: "INDIA IS PRIMED FOR VOICE"
(A market analysis of voice adoption opportunity)
================================================================================

A1. MARKET OPPORTUNITY
----------------------
Mobile-First
  - 500M+ smartphone users; fastest growing market
  - Mobile is the primary internet device for the majority
  - Small screens make typing friction-heavy
  - Voice reduces interaction friction by 60%+

Multilingual Reality
  - 22 official languages, hundreds of dialects
  - Regional language typing is 3x slower than English
  - Keyboard layouts are complex for non-Latin scripts
  - Users are 2x more likely to speak than type in regional languages

A2. EXISTING VOICE BEHAVIOR IN INDIA
------------------------------------
  - WhatsApp: voice notes are mainstream communication -- 100M+ voice
    messages daily
  - YouTube: voice search helps discovery -- 30% of searches are voice-based
    in India
  - Google Assistant: utility tasks are normalized -- weather, alarms,
    directions, reminders
  - Alexa: conversational interactions are trusted -- smart home adoption is
    growing 40% YoY

Is India ready for voice?  YES -- India is a strong voice market. Users already
use voice for communication, search and utility tasks. The opportunity for
ChatGPT is to reduce friction through better discoverability, trust and support
for Indian-language use cases.

A3. WHY VOICE ADOPTION IS LOW ON CHATGPT (UX gaps + competitor lessons)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
ChatGPT Voice UX Audit -- four gaps:
  1. Discoverability        -- voice isn't strongly surfaced
  2. No contextual nudges   -- missing "Try voice" prompts
  3. Trust & uncertainty    -- recording concerns, accent and language doubts
  4. Weak onboarding        -- no clear value explanation

Visual evidence: ChatGPT mobile input bar. The voice (mic) icon is small, sits
among the other icons (+, Search, Reason, voice-mode button) and is "easy to
miss". ChatGPT's voice entry is visually subtle.

What competitors do better:
  Product           | UX Pattern                   | PM Lesson
  ------------------+------------------------------+--------------------
  WhatsApp          | One-tap voice note           | Ultra-low friction
  Google Assistant  | Voice-first default          | Discoverability
  YouTube           | Prominent voice search icon  | Contextual utility
  Alexa             | Conversational feedback      | Trust + clarity

Competitors make voice visible, simple and trustworthy. ChatGPT treats it as
optional.

Key product insights:
  - Demand exists. Activation is broken.
  - There is no compelling "why voice?" trigger.
  - Availability != habit adoption.

A4. WHY THIS PRODUCT OUTCOME MATTERS
------------------------------------
  - Engagement: more voice usage drives higher session frequency and
    time-on-app
  - Retention: voice-first users show stronger habit formation and lower churn
  - Differentiation: voice becomes a competitive moat in India's mobile-first
    market

"Voice is not a feature -- it's a market entry strategy for India."

A5. METRIC TREE (from M1)
-------------------------
Goal: Increase ChatGPT Voice Usage (North Star goal)

  Voice Usage = Active Users  x  Voice Adoption Rate  x  Voice Usage Frequency

  Active Users (people who can potentially use voice)
    = New Users (first-time) + Returning Users (previously active)
        New Users       = New Installs (India) + Install-to-Signup Rate
        Returning Users = Reactivated Users + Existing Active Users

  Voice Adoption Rate (% of active users who try voice)
    = Discovery (reach) x Activation (success)
        Discovery  -> Voice Icon Visibility (% users exposed)
        Activation -> First Voice Completion (completion rate)

  Voice Usage Frequency (how often adopters use voice)
    = Sessions/User (voice sessions per active user)
      x Queries/Session (voice queries per session)
        Sessions/User   -> Days Active (voice days per user) x Sessions per
                           Active Day
        Queries/Session -> Queries per Session x Multi-turn Depth
                           (follow-up rate)

  NORTH STAR METRIC (M1): Weekly Active Voice Users (India). It measures real
  adoption and repeat usage over time.
  (The PRD narrows this to Weekly Active Voice Learners, WAVL. See Part C.)

B5. PROBLEM FRAMING CANVAS -- ChatGPT Voice for Bharat Learners
---------------------------------------------------------------
CORE INSIGHT: Voice has a retention problem, not an awareness problem.

- What is the true problem?
  Bharat Learners know ChatGPT voice exists but don't use it for study tasks.
  Nothing in context makes voice feel like the right tool for the moment.

- Who are the customers facing the problem?
  Bharat Learners (18-24, Tier-2/3, regional-language first) who actively use
  ChatGPT but default to typing even though they know about voice.

- How do we know it is a real problem?
  Survey (n=20): 80% aware, 85% tried, only 4/20 use voice often; 70% prefer
  typing. Verbatim: "The voice feature is not very inviting -- I only tried it
  when I had to test usability."

- What is the value generated by solving it?
  For target customers: faster, hands-free study help in their own language;
    less friction to learn, practise English and solve doubts without typing.
  For the business: activates the next 100M Bharat users for ChatGPT; voice
    becomes a defensible India moat against Gemini's voice-first lead.

- Why solve it now?
  Users already use voice elsewhere (YouTube, WhatsApp), but ChatGPT is failing
  to turn that habit into study behaviour. The window to build a voice habit
  before competitors lock it in is narrowing.

WHY NOW: India has ~690M smartphone users. Typing in regional scripts is ~3x

HYPOTHESIS: Bharat Learners under-use ChatGPT Voice for three reasons:
  (1) there is no prompt in context,
  (2) they are unsure how it handles accents and privacy, and
  (3) there is no clear reason to choose voice over typing.

C5. SOLUTION
------------
Build Smart Voice Suggestions: when a learner asks for help with a task where
talking works better than typing, ChatGPT offers Voice Mode right in the chat.

Options considered (RICE):
  Option                    | Description                       | Reach  | Impact | Confidence | Effort | Priority
  --------------------------+-----------------------------------+--------+--------+------------+--------+------------------------------
  A. Smart Voice            | Offer Voice Mode during tasks     | High   | High   | High       | Low    | HIGHEST: build now
     Suggestions            | such as interview prep, spoken    |        |        |            |        |
                            | English practice and viva prep    |        |        |            |        |
  B. Voice Preview Mode     | A 30-second guided voice demo     | Medium | Medium | Medium     | Low    | Medium: candidate for v2,
                            | before a full session             |        |        |            |        | can sit on top of A
  C. Voice Learning         | Structured 7-day voice challenges | Medium | Medium | Medium     | High   | Low: revisit once a habit
     Challenges             | for speaking, interviews and      |        |        |            |        | loop is proven
                            | concepts                          |        |        |            |        |

Why option A: it targets the main research finding head-on: users don't know
WHEN to use voice. It meets them inside the typed chat they already use and
reuses the existing Voice Mode. It scored highest on RICE for the lowest
effort, and it gives a clean exposure event for A/B testing.

How it works:
  1. DETECT: a lightweight classifier tags the learner's typed query as
     voice-friendly: mock interview, speaking practice, viva or oral revision,
     or pronunciation.
  2. SUGGEST: under ChatGPT's normal text answer, a card appears: "Practice
     with Voice: speak your answers out loud and get instant feedback on your
     confidence and accuracy", with a [Try Voice Practice] button. The text
     answer always comes first and is never blocked.
  3. START IN CONTEXT: one tap opens Voice Mode already set for the task (e.g.
     Interview Practice opens with its first question), so the user doesn't
     repeat themselves.
  4. CLOSE THE LOOP: when the session ends, a summary shows duration,
     questions practised and progress toward a streak goal. It offers
     [Practice Again] or [Back to Chat] and suggests a next voice activity.

USER FLOW ("One tap turns a typed study task into a voice session"):
  Types a query -> Gets text answer -> [Voice-friendly task?]
     no  -> Continues typing
     yes -> Suggestion card appears
              dismissed           -> Continues typing
              taps Try Voice Practice -> Voice session, task preset
                 -> Speaks answers, gets feedback -> Session summary
                 -> Practice again or next activity
The text answer always comes first. Voice is offered, never forced. If the
user dismisses the card or the task isn't voice-friendly, they keep typing as
they do today.

C6. REQUIREMENTS
----------------
The MVP covers every P0 item. P1 items ship in the same release if capacity
allows, or in the next one.

Functional requirements:
  FR-1  [P0] Detect voice-friendly learning intent in typed queries: mock
        interview, spoken English, viva or oral revision, pronunciation.
        AC: Precision >=85% on a labelled set of >=1,000 Indian learner
        queries (English and Hinglish). Text replies get no slower in any way
        users notice.
  FR-2  [P0] Show a Smart Voice Suggestion card below the text answer.
        AC: Card has a task-specific title, one line on why voice helps, and a
        Try Voice Practice button. The text answer always renders first.
  FR-3  [P0] Open Voice Mode with the task already set.
        AC: One tap opens a session labelled with the task (e.g. "Interview
        Practice"). The assistant speaks first with a relevant prompt, and the
        chat context carries over.
  FR-4  [P0] Let users dismiss and turn off suggestions.
        AC: Every card has a dismiss (x) and a "Don't suggest voice" option.
        Turning it off works at once and can be reversed in Settings.
  FR-5  [P0] Limit how often suggestions appear.
        AC: At most 1 suggestion per conversation and 3 per user per week.
        After 3 dismissals in a row, suggestions pause for 14 days.
  FR-6  [P0] Reassure users on privacy before the first session.
        AC: On first use, a short line covers how voice data is handled, with
        a link to the policy. Uses existing policy only, with no new data
        collection.
  FR-7  [P0] Handle a denied mic permission smoothly.
        AC: If the mic is blocked, explain why it's needed, offer a link to
        settings, and return to text with no dead end.
  FR-8  [P1] Show a session summary screen.
        AC: Shows duration, number of questions practised, one improvement tip
        and streak progress, with Practice Again and Back to Chat.
  FR-9  [P1] Suggest a next voice activity.
        AC: The summary suggests one related activity (e.g. "Practise HR
        questions next").
  FR-10 [P1] Easy-to-find speed and accent controls.
        AC: Speed and accent controls can be reached in one tap from the
        active voice session screen. (Addresses R16's friction quote.)
  FR-11 [P1] Localized suggestion copy.
        AC: Card copy available in English and Hindi first, then the top 3
        regional languages by segment usage.

Non-functional requirements:
  - Performance and reliability: intent detection adds no wait to text
    replies. Voice sessions start in <2s on a mid-range Android phone over 4G.
    If the classifier or voice service fails, the card is hidden and text chat
    carries on.
  - Privacy and accessibility: no new audio or transcript storage beyond the
    current Voice Mode policy; India's DPDP Act rules on notice and consent
    are followed. The card works with TalkBack and VoiceOver and meets
    WCAG AA.
  - Control: a server-side kill switch can turn off suggestions by region,
    language or cohort without an app release.

C7. EDGE CASES
--------------
  Scenario                                   | Expected behaviour
  -------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------
  Query is ambiguous (e.g. "explain          | Show the card only above the confidence
  interview questions for testers")          | threshold; otherwise show nothing.
  User is in a noisy or shared space         | Card subtext suggests headphones. The voice
                                             | session shows a "noisy room" hint if input
                                             | quality drops.
  User switches to Hindi or Hinglish         | Session continues in the detected language.
  mid-session                                | The summary is written in the language the
                                             | user spoke most.
  Network drops during a voice session       | Save the transcript so far and offer to
                                             | continue in text without losing context.

C8. EXPERIMENT PLAN
-------------------
Randomized A/B test for 4 weeks. Ship only if voice start rate AND D7 voice
retention both improve and no guardrail is breached.
  - Who's in it: India users aged 18-24 in Tier-2/3 regions who send at least
    one eligible learning query, split 50/50 at user level into Control (no
    card) and Treatment.
  - Primary metric: voice start rate among ELIGIBLE users in both arms (so
    control isn't undercounted). Secondary: sessions per user, D7 voice
    retention, WAVL.
  - Length and size: 4 weeks, so D7 has time to mature and exam weeks and
    weekends even out. Sized to detect a 2 pp lift at 95% confidence and 80%
    power.
  - Instrumentation: suggestion eligible, shown, tapped, dismissed; voice
    session started, completed. Each event is tagged with intent type,
    language and arm.
  - Decision rules:
      SHIP    if primary metrics improve and guardrails hold.
      ITERATE if taps rise but D7 stays flat (fix the in-session experience).
      KILL    if opt-outs exceed 5% or text satisfaction drops.

C9. LAUNCH PLAN (four phases; each needs the previous phase's gate to pass)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Internal dogfood (2 weeks): India staff + a student beta panel.
     Gate: classifier precision >=85%, no P0 bugs, privacy copy approved by
     Legal and Trust & Safety.
  2. A/B test at 10% of eligible users (4 weeks).
     Gate: primary metrics clear the targets and guardrails hold.
  3. Ramp to 50%, then 100% in India; add Hindi, then regional-language card
     copy.
     Gate: results hold at scale, and voice infrastructure cost per WAVL stays
     within budget.
  4. v2 bets: add Voice Preview Mode on top, then test Voice Learning
     Challenges with users who have built a habit.

C10. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
--------------------------
  Risk                                       | Likelihood | Mitigation
  -------------------------------------------+------------+--------------------------------------
  Suggestions feel pushy to the 71% who      | High       | Frequency limits, easy dismiss and
  prefer typing                              |            | opt-out, opt-out guardrail, contextual
                                             |            | triggers only
  Speaking aloud feels awkward in hostels    | Medium     | Copy that suggests headphones;
  and libraries                              |            | whisper/low-volume mode is a v2
                                             |            | candidate
  Accent or Hinglish recognition fails and   | Medium     | Start with tasks where recognition
  trust drops after the first try            |            | already performs well; flag poor
                                             |            | sessions; share data with speech team
  More voice use raises infrastructure cost  | Medium     | Track cost per WAVL, cap session
                                             |            | length in the MVP, use the kill switch

C11. DECISIONS TAKEN
--------------------
  - Prompt before model: v1 changes WHEN voice is offered, not how speech
    recognition works. Accent and recognition work stays with the speech team.
  - Suggestions, not a default switch: text stays the default and voice is
    always opt-in, so we don't push away the 71% who prefer typing.
  - English and Hinglish first: regional-language card copy follows in the
    ramp phase, once the classifier proves reliable.

D3. KEY TAKEAWAYS
-----------------
  1. India is voice-ready (WhatsApp, YouTube, Assistant habits); ChatGPT's
     voice is simply under-surfaced and lacks a "why now" trigger.
  2. The target is Bharat Learners (18-24, Tier-2/3, vernacular-first): ~7M
     weekly ChatGPT users today, ~22M headroom.
  3. The bottleneck is between trial and habit (an activation/retention
     cliff), not awareness or demand.
  4. The fix is behavioural/UX (when to offer voice), not model-level (how
     speech is recognised).
  5. Smart Voice Suggestions = detect voice-friendly study intent -> suggest
     below the text answer -> one-tap task-preset voice session -> summary +
     streak + next activity.
  6. Success = WAVL +25%, voice start rate >=15%, D7 voice retention +5 pp,
     with guardrails: opt-out <=5% and no rise in text thumbs-down.
  7. Safeguards against being pushy: max 1 per conversation and 3 per week,
     14-day pause after 3 dismissals in a row, easy opt-out, kill switch,
     text always first.
